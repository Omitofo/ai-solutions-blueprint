## 05 — Understand the costs

**Section title**  
Understand the costs

**Supporting line**  
What tokens are, how agents use them, and what you can roughly expect.

**Intro paragraph**  
AI systems mainly charge by the amount of text they read and write. Those units are called tokens. Once you understand them, the economics become much clearer.

### What is a token?

| Quantity | Rough equivalent |
|----------|------------------|
| 1 token | ~0.5–0.75 words in Spanish |
| 1,000 tokens | 500–750 words |
| 10,000 tokens | 5,000–7,500 words |
| 100,000 tokens | 50,000–75,000 words |
| 1,000,000 tokens | 500,000–750,000 words |

Input tokens (what the model reads) and output tokens (what it writes) are priced differently.

### Scenario examples

**Scenario A — Light email volume**  
1,000 emails/month processed by an agent.  
Pure model cost is often only a few euros per month.

**Scenario B — Active administrative user**  
Someone working with the assistant throughout the day.  
Monthly consumption stays in a very manageable range for most small teams.

**Scenario C — Higher volume or multi-step agents**  
More emails, longer documents, or agents that take several steps.  
Cost rises mainly with complexity and repeated context — not just with volume.

### Important note
One task can consume tokens several times (reading, tool use, reasoning, drafting, checking).  
The final answer you see is only part of the work the system did.

### What actually matters
We track cost per successful task, not just total tokens.  
That is the number that tells you whether the system is worth it.
